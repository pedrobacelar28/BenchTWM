\title{BenchTWM: An Offline Benchmark for World Models and\\
Model-Free Reinforcement Learning on Structured Decision Data}
\author{Technical Specification}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
BenchTWM is a reproducible offline reinforcement learning (RL) benchmark that asks whether model-based methods, learned world models used for planning, outperform model-free offline RL when every policy must be learned from logged decisions alone. It integrates two healthcare-oriented simulators behind one adapter and one logged-transition schema: ICU-Sepsis, a finite MDP parameterized from MIMIC-III-derived assets, and EpiCare, a procedurally generated partially observable treatment benchmark. It supplies fixed logged datasets with exact behavior propensities, four model-free methods (BC, DQN, IQL, CQL), four model-based methods (three world-model families with cross-entropy-method planning and a DreamerV3-style agent), a single offline training loop with validation-based model selection, direct simulator evaluation, and importance-sampling off-policy evaluation (OPE). This document specifies the observation, action, dataset, optimization, and evaluation contracts. It reports no empirical results and makes no clinical claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and Design Principles}
\label{sec:scope}

Offline RL optimizes a policy from a fixed log of decisions. BenchTWM keeps four objects distinct: the training log, a held-out validation log used for model selection, simulator episodes used for direct evaluation, and a held-out OPE log. A run optimizes on \code{train.npz}, selects its checkpoint by validation loss on \code{val.npz}, evaluates that checkpoint in fresh simulator episodes, and computes OPE on \code{ope\_test.npz}. Neither the simulator nor OPE influences training or checkpoint selection, mirroring settings such as hospitals or companies in which candidate policies cannot be tried on live subjects.

The benchmark has four design goals:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A single question.} Methods are grouped into a model-free and a model-based family, and results report the \emph{planning gain} of each model-based method over the best model-free method on the same dataset, together with its computational cost.
    \item \textbf{Offline-only operation.} Dataset provenance, exact behavior propensities, trajectory identity, and split separation are first-class artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Extensibility.} Environments and algorithms are registries with explicit contracts; contract tests run every registered algorithm on every registered environment.
    \item \textbf{Comparable reporting without false equivalence.} Return, success, length, action-change statistics, and cost are standardized; clinical semantics remain environment-specific and datasets are never averaged together.
\end{enumerate}

The benchmark is an engineering artifact, not a clinical decision-support system. Both environments are simulators; neither simulator performance nor OPE estimates are treatment recommendations.

\section{Notation and System Contract}
\label{sec:contract}

At step $t$ an environment adapter returns an observation $o_t\in\R^{d}$ (\code{float32}), an information dictionary, and a binary action mask $m_t\in\{0,1\}^{A}$. The policy selects $a_t\in\{0,\ldots,A-1\}$ with $m_{t,a_t}=1$. A logged transition is
\begin{equation}
    (o_t, a_t, r_t, o_{t+1}, d_t, m_t, m_{t+1}),
\end{equation}
with terminal indicator $d_t$. The adapter also exposes a diagnostic state identifier $s_t$, never an agent input, and named per-step metrics $q_t$.

Adapters are registered by environment id and instantiated with \code{make\_env(config, seed)}. Each exports an \code{EnvSpec} with dimensions, feature and action names, action geometry, metric names, and a primary metric; the spec is persisted with every dataset and evaluation.

Every policy, whether a behavior policy, a reference baseline, or a learned agent, implements one contract:
\begin{align}
    \code{action\_probabilities}(o_t,\mathrm{info},\mathrm{deterministic}) &\rightarrow p_t\in[0,1]^{A},\\
    \code{act}(o_t,\mathrm{info},\mathrm{deterministic}) &\rightarrow a_t,
\end{align}
plus \code{reset\_episode} and \code{observe} hooks for stateful policies. Dataset collection samples from $p_t$ and records its exact log-probability; OPE reads $p_t$ as the target probability; deployment takes its argmax.

\subsection{ICU-Sepsis}
\label{sec:icu}

ICU-Sepsis is an MDP constructed from approximately 17{,}000 MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories \cite{choudhary2024icu}. The packaged environment has 716 states and 25 actions, with transition and reward matrices, state-cluster centroids, state-level SOFA scores, a clinician (expert) policy, and state-dependent admissible actions. BenchTWM wraps the packaged simulator; it does not re-estimate the MDP.

\paragraph{Observations.} The agent observes the 47-dimensional centroid of the current state's cluster, named after the AI Clinician features used to build the MDP (4 binary, 32 z-scored, and 11 log-transformed and z-scored variables). Because the MDP is tabular, only 716 distinct vectors occur, and binary variables appear as cluster averages.

\paragraph{Actions.} An action decodes as $a=5f+v$ with fluid bin $f$ and vasopressor bin $v$ in $\{0,\ldots,4\}$. Inadmissible actions are masked. Action distance is $|f-f'|+|v-v'|$; a change is abrupt if either bin moves by more than one.

\paragraph{Outcomes and behavior policies.} The adapter reports \code{sofa\_score} per step; success is a positive episode return (survival). Behavior policies are uniform over admissible actions, the packaged clinician frequencies renormalized on admissible actions, an $\epsilon$-mixture of the two, and the deterministic value-iteration optimum of the packaged MDP.

\subsection{EpiCare}
\label{sec:epicare}

EpiCare is a configurable benchmark for dynamic treatment regimes with partial observability, adverse events, and OPE failure modes \cite{hargrave2024epicare}. The default configuration has 16 latent diseases, 16 treatments, 8 observed symptoms, a maximum of 8 visits, and per-patient modifiers. Each disease has a multivariate Gaussian symptom distribution with an orthogonally rotated covariance; observed symptoms are logistic-transformed, shifted by treatment and patient modifiers, and rounded to one decimal. The latent disease is exported only as the diagnostic \code{state\_id}.

The \code{environment\_seed} fixes the generated POMDP; the reset seed samples a patient. Rewards combine treatment and symptom costs with a remission reward ($+64$) and an adverse-event penalty ($-64$), both terminal. Reported metrics are symptom burden, remission, adverse event, and time to remission; success is remission. Behavior policies are the upstream Random, ClinicalTrial (SMART), Standard of Care (adaptive, deterministic), and Oracle (reads the latent disease; a privileged reference). The vendored copy fixes the quasi-Monte Carlo seed of the multivariate-normal CDF used by these reference policies, which upstream drew from the global random state.

The EpiCare paper evaluates BC, DQN, AWAC, EDAC, TD3+BC, IQL, and CQL implementations specific to that study; BenchTWM neither vendors nor reproduces them.

\section{Offline Dataset Specification}
\label{sec:data}

A dataset is identified by environment, behavior policy, and size tier (\code{dev} or \code{final}). Collection writes \code{train.npz}, \code{val.npz}, and \code{ope\_test.npz} with disjoint episode ids and reset-seed ranges, plus \code{metadata.json}. Every logged action is sampled from the behavior distribution, and
\begin{equation}
    \ell_t^{\behavior}=\log\behavior(a_t\mid o_t)
\end{equation}
is stored exactly. Collection is deterministic given its configuration.

\section{Implemented Algorithms}
\label{sec:methods}

All methods consume $o_t$ and respect the mask: for logits or Q-values $u(o)$, $\widetilde u_a=u_a$ if $m_a=1$ and $-10^9$ otherwise. Model-free networks are MLPs of configurable width and depth.

\subsection{Model-free methods}

\paragraph{BC.} Minimizes $-\log\policy_\theta(a_t\mid o_t,m_t)$ over logged actions.

\paragraph{DQN.} With a target network $Q_{\bar\theta}$ updated every $K$ steps by $\bar\theta\leftarrow\tau\theta+(1-\tau)\bar\theta$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DQN}}=\Big[Q_\theta(o_t,a_t)-\big(r_t+\gamma(1-d_t)\max_{a':m_{t+1,a'}=1}Q_{\bar\theta}(o_{t+1},a')\big)\Big]^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{IQL.} Twin critics regress $r_t+\gamma(1-d_t)V_\psi(o_{t+1})$; the value fits the $\tau$-expectile of $\min(Q_1,Q_2)$; the actor minimizes $-\exp\big(\beta(\min Q-V)\big)\log\policy_\theta(a_t\mid o_t)$ with clipped weights \cite{kostrikov2022iql}.

\paragraph{CQL.} Twin critics regress $r_t+\gamma(1-d_t)\max_{a'}\min(Q_1,Q_2)(o_{t+1},a')$ plus $\alpha\big(\log\sum_{a:m_a=1}e^{Q(o_t,a)}-Q(o_t,a_t)\big)$ \cite{kumar2020cql}; deployment is greedy on $\min(Q_1,Q_2)$.

\subsection{World-model families with CEM planning}
\label{sec:worldmodels}

Each family conditions on an action embedding $e(a)$ and predicts a next state, a reward $\hat r_t$, and a continuation logit $\hat c_t$, with auxiliary losses $\operatorname{MSE}(\hat r_t,r_t)$ and $\operatorname{BCE}(\hat c_t,1-d_t)$.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Raw model}: predicts $\hat o_{t+1}$ directly with $\operatorname{MSE}(\hat o_{t+1},o_{t+1})$.
    \item \textbf{Latent model}: encodes $h_t=E(o_t)$, predicts $\hat h_{t+1}=F(h_t,e(a_t))$, and regresses a stop-gradient EMA target $\bar E(o_{t+1})$, $\bar\theta_E\leftarrow\delta\bar\theta_E+(1-\delta)\theta_E$.
    \item \textbf{Reconstructive model}: as the latent model but with target $E(o_{t+1})$ and an added reconstruction loss $\operatorname{MSE}(D(\hat h_{t+1}),o_{t+1})$.
\end{itemize}
Validation additionally reports open-loop $k$-step rollout errors of state, reward, continuation, and discounted return for $k\in\{1,3,5,\min(H,10)\}$; these are model-fit diagnostics, not value estimates.

\paragraph{Categorical CEM.} At decision time the planner keeps independent categorical distributions $p_{0:H-1}$, with $p_0$ restricted to admissible actions, samples $K$ sequences, scores each by
\begin{equation}
 S(a_{0:H-1})=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t\Big(\prod_{j<t}\sigma(\hat c_j)\Big)\hat r_t,
\end{equation}
refits $p_t\leftarrow\operatorname{normalize}(\alpha p_t+(1-\alpha)\hat p_t)$ from the top $E$ sequences for $I$ iterations, and executes the first action of the best sequence. Its first-step distribution is exposed for OPE but labelled diagnostic, since it depends on sampled plans.

\subsection{Offline DreamerV3-style agent}
\label{sec:dreamer}

A PyTorch port of the vector-observation, discrete-action path of DreamerV3 \cite{hafner2023dreamerv3}: a blocked RSSM with categorical stochastic states, balanced KL with free nats, symlog reconstruction, two-hot reward and value heads, continuation prediction, 15-step imagined actor--critic learning with lambda returns and slow-value regularization, replay-value learning, and the source AGC--RMS--momentum optimizer. It trains on batches of 16 sequences of length 64, carrying the recurrent state across consecutive chunks of an episode. The action mask is applied only to deployed actor logits.

\section{Optimization and Model Selection}
\label{sec:training}

One loop trains every method for a fixed number of gradient steps (5{,}000 by default, batch 256 transitions or 16 sequences). Every 250 steps it computes the method's loss on the full validation split; the lowest value selects the deployed checkpoint, and 20 checks without improvement stop training early. Gradient norms are clipped at 10 for all but Dreamer, which uses its own adaptive clipping. The simulator is never called during this phase, and hyperparameter search likewise minimizes validation loss.

Configurations are resolved from layers: a shared protocol, per-algorithm defaults, environment settings, optional (environment, algorithm) overrides, and command-line overrides, each of which may carry tier-specific values.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Direct simulator evaluation}

The selected checkpoint is deployed deterministically for 500 episodes with evaluation seeds shared by all methods. Reported are mean return, success, and length; action stability and abrupt-change rate under the environment's action distance; per-step metric means; episode outcomes; and cost: action latency, planner calls, and planner model evaluations.

\subsection{Trajectory-wise OPE}
\label{sec:ope}

For held-out trajectory $i$ of length $T_i$, with $\rho_{i,t}=\policy(a_{i,t}\mid o_{i,t})/\behavior(a_{i,t}\mid o_{i,t})$, $W_{i,t}=\prod_{k\le t}\rho_{i,k}$, $W_i=W_{i,T_i-1}$, and $G_i=\sum_t\gamma^tr_{i,t}$,
\begin{align}
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{IS}}&=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_iW_iG_i, &
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{WIS}}&=\textstyle\sum_iW_iG_i/\sum_iW_i,\\
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{PDIS}}&=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_i\sum_t\gamma^tW_{i,t}r_{i,t}, &
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{WPDIS}}&=\textstyle\sum_t\gamma^t\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t}r_{i,t}}{\sum_iW_{i,t}},
\end{align}
where WPDIS keeps finished trajectories in later denominators with their final weight and zero reward. Seeded trajectory bootstrap gives 95\% intervals. Diagnostics (target support, zero-weight rate, trajectory ESS, maximum log weight) map to a reliability label; a deterministic target often yields low ESS, which is reported as insufficient support rather than as a ranking.

\subsection{Planning gain}

For dataset $\dataset$ with best model-free method $b(\dataset)$ by mean return across seeds, the planning gain of model-based method $k$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_k(\dataset)=\bar R_k(\dataset)-\bar R_{b(\dataset)}(\dataset),
\end{equation}
reported with the analogous success difference and the method's training time, evaluation time, action latency, and planner model evaluations. Gains are never averaged across datasets.

\section{Reproducibility and Artifacts}
\label{sec:artifacts}

Each resolved configuration is hashed into its run directory, which holds the configuration, a manifest with provenance (code revision, library versions, devices) and lifecycle status (completed, failed, or interrupted), the validation history, best and last checkpoints, evaluation tables, OPE outputs, and a single scalar summary. Aggregation produces per-method statistics and planning gains per dataset.

\section{Limitations and Non-Claims}
\label{sec:limitations}

Shared field names do not imply a shared clinical scale: ICU SOFA and EpiCare symptom burden differ in origin and meaning. ICU-Sepsis observations are cluster centroids of a tabular MDP rather than individual patient records, and EpiCare symptoms are homogeneous synthetic features; neither is a heterogeneous tabular dataset. EpiCare's hidden disease is excluded from inputs, and no model recovers unidentifiable state. The world models are not calibrated causal models. Importance-sampling OPE is vulnerable to support mismatch and weight degeneracy on multi-step trajectories. No simulator result validates a policy for clinical use.
